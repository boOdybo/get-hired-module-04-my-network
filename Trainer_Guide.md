# Module 04 — Trainer Guide & Implementation Notes

## Session identity

- **Day:** 4 — MY NETWORK
- **Formal session:** AI-Powered Networking & LinkedIn Optimization
- **Official window:** 12:00–14:00
- **Official duration:** 120 minutes
- **Tools named in source:** LinkedIn, ChatGPT, Grammarly
- **Official activity:** LinkedIn Makeover
- **Direct AI Tools output:** LinkedIn Headline and About section draft (AI-assisted)

## Teaching purpose

Students should use AI to improve the clarity and relevance of their professional presence while retaining ownership of their identity, evidence, and professional boundaries. The learning sequence is:

**Profile facts → ChatGPT Headline/About drafts → Grammarly language review → human personalisation → peer/trainer review → responsible profile decision.**

AI-assisted opportunity discovery and connection messages are included, but they must be checked and personalised rather than copied automatically.

## Suggested 120-minute pacing

This is a trainer-designed distribution. The source fixes the 12:00–14:00 window but does not assign minutes to individual topics.

| Stage | Suggested time |
|---|---:|
| Opening and learning outcomes | 5 min |
| Networking mindset and LinkedIn profile foundations | 15 min |
| ChatGPT Headline and About demonstration | 25 min |
| Personalised connection request practice | 15 min |
| Grammarly review workflow | 10 min |
| Opportunity search, verification, privacy | 15 min |
| LinkedIn Makeover: draft, personalise, review, peer vote | 30 min |
| Evidence check and close | 5 min |
| **Total** | **120 min** |

## Before class

1. Check that the trainer device can open LinkedIn, ChatGPT, and Grammarly.
2. Test Grammarly on the browser and text field that will be used. Its extension may not support every website or field.
3. Prepare a fictional graduate profile for the demonstration. Do not use a student's private profile or confidential information.
4. Prepare a no-login/offline example with a fictional Headline and About in case students cannot access one of the tools.
5. Tell students not to enter passwords, identity numbers, confidential employer data, or private details about other people into AI tools.
6. Explain that publishing the revised profile during the session is not required; the required output is a draft.

## Core teaching sequence

### 1. Networking mindset

Explain the source's definition: networking is building genuine professional relationships, not collecting contacts. Discuss “give first, receive later” as a service mindset. Compare warm networking (existing connection or shared context) with cold networking (new professional contact). Emphasise that cold outreach should still be relevant and respectful.

### 2. LinkedIn profile foundations

For fresh graduates, profile evidence can include education, projects, internships, volunteering, coursework, and skills that can be demonstrated. Do not imply that students need formal employment to write a credible profile.

Explain the difference:
- **Headline:** a concise professional identifier below the name.
- **About:** a broader professional narrative where the student can explain motivation, skills, evidence, and direction.

The deck's Headline formula (field/target role + relevant skills + value/direction) and five-part About structure (opening, focus, evidence, direction, invitation) are instructional frameworks, not official LinkedIn formulas.

### 3. ChatGPT Headline demonstration

Preserve the source prompt exactly:

```text
Write a LinkedIn headline for a fresh graduate in [field] targeting [sector]. Make it specific and value-focused.
```

Demonstration steps:
1. Identify the field and target sector.
2. Add verified skills and one real project or experience.
3. Ask ChatGPT for multiple alternatives.
4. Check accuracy, specificity, relevance, value, and authenticity.
5. Reject any title that exaggerates seniority, expertise, or experience.
6. Ask for a more concise alternative if needed.

Suggested refinement prompt:

```text
Revise option [number] to make it clearer and more specific. Use only the skills and evidence I provided. Do not imply professional experience or advanced expertise that I have not demonstrated.
```

### 4. About section demonstration

Use the five-part instructional structure:
1. Opening: field or current professional identity.
2. Focus: topics or problems of interest.
3. Evidence: a real project, internship, volunteering, coursework, or experience.
4. Direction: target sector or role.
5. Invitation: a low-pressure professional invitation to connect.

Additional prompt (trainer-designed):

```text
Act as a LinkedIn profile writing coach. Help me draft an About section as a fresh graduate.
My field: [field]
My target sector or role: [sector/role]
My real skills: [skills I can demonstrate]
My evidence: [project, internship, volunteering, coursework, or experience]
My professional interests: [interests]
Write in a clear, professional, natural voice. Do not invent qualifications, experience, achievements, or metrics. If important information is missing, ask me questions before drafting.
```

Show students how to identify generic language and unsupported claims. A good draft should be specific, accurate, and recognisably theirs.

### 5. Connection request messages

Preserve the source's comparison: personalised vs. generic. Teach students to include a genuine context, a specific reason, a brief introduction, and a respectful ask. Do not teach them to invent common connections, pretend to have read a post, or ask for a job immediately.

Suggested prompt:

```text
Write a concise LinkedIn connection request to [person's name].
Why I am contacting them: [a specific post, event, shared university, or professional topic I have verified].
My context: [my field and current career direction].
What I appreciated or want to learn: [specific point].
Keep the tone respectful, natural, and personal. Do not invent a shared connection, claim to have read content I have not read, ask for a job immediately, or use exaggerated praise.
```

### 6. Grammarly review

The source requires polishing LinkedIn profile text for clarity and professionalism. The reliable workflow is:
1. Draft the text in a document or safe text field.
2. Use Grammarly Editor or the browser extension if the target field is supported.
3. Review each suggestion for correctness, clarity, concision, tone, and meaning.
4. Reject any suggestion that changes the student's meaning or overstates their skills.
5. Paste the approved text back into LinkedIn.

Grammarly's current help pages list Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge for its browser extension, but support can vary by website and text field. Do not promise that Grammarly will appear in every LinkedIn field. Test the trainer device before class; use Grammarly Editor as fallback.

### 7. AI-assisted opportunity discovery

Use the source's scope: professional communities, events, and groups. Example prompt:

```text
Help me identify professional communities, events, and LinkedIn groups relevant to [sector/field] in [city/region].
For each suggestion, provide: name and official website or LinkedIn page, why it may be relevant to a fresh graduate, the type of people or activities I may find there, and what I should verify before joining or attending.
Do not invent group names, links, event dates, or membership requirements. Clearly label anything you cannot verify.
```

Require verification of existence, recent activity, relevance, access requirements, and safety. Use LinkedIn Search categories such as People, Groups, and Events when available. Check the organisation's official page before presenting an opportunity as real or current.

### 8. Privacy and professional boundaries

Discuss what is appropriate to share (education, skills, public projects, professional interests) and what to protect (identity documents, passwords, confidential work/client data, and private information about others). Explain LinkedIn public-profile visibility at a high level and invite students to review settings privately. Do not ask them to show private account settings to the class.

## Official activity — LinkedIn Makeover

**Objective:** Produce and improve a Headline and About section using ChatGPT while preserving truthful personal detail.

**Format:** Individual drafting with trainer/peer review and peer voting.

**Suggested allocation:** 30 minutes within the 120-minute session.

**Steps:**
1. Students draft a Headline and About using the ChatGPT prompts.
2. Students manually improve the drafts with personal details.
3. Trainer and peers review the drafts.
4. The best About section is read aloud and receives peer-vote XP.

**Expected output:** Original Headline, AI-assisted draft, manually revised Headline, About draft, and review notes.

**Suggested peer-review criteria (trainer-designed, not official source XP rules):**
- Truthfulness: every claim is supportable.
- Specificity: field and skills are clear.
- Relevance: content aligns with target sector/role.
- Clarity: language is natural and professional.
- Personal voice: text sounds like the student, not a generic template.

Do not invent an XP amount. Follow the programme organiser's scoring rules. Respect students who prefer not to read personal details publicly; they may share a redacted excerpt or a fictional practice version.

## Assessment evidence

| Evidence | What to check |
|---|---|
| Headline options | Specific, value-focused, and truthful. |
| About draft | Includes real evidence and professional direction. |
| Human revision | Visible changes and reasons for accepting/rejecting AI suggestions. |
| Connection request | Genuine personalisation and respectful ask. |
| Opportunity search | Official source and current status checked. |
| Privacy decision | Student can identify what should remain private. |

## Daily deliverable attribution

- **LinkedIn Headline and About section draft (AI-assisted):** direct AI Tools output.
- **Written networking introduction script (60 seconds):** whole-day deliverable, primarily Business English.
- **Follow-up email template (personalised):** whole-day deliverable, primarily Business English/professional communication.
- **Networking contact card (name, field, goal):** whole-day deliverable, primarily networking simulation/soft skills.

## Fallbacks

- **No LinkedIn account:** draft using the profile mock-up and the worksheet; do not require account creation during class.
- **No ChatGPT access:** use the prepared fictional output and have students write/edit the prompt manually.
- **Grammarly not supported in LinkedIn:** use Grammarly Editor or a human review checklist.
- **No internet:** use the pre-prepared list of verified sample opportunities supplied by the trainer, with the source date visible.
- **No work experience:** use a real university project, volunteering, coursework, or personal project.
